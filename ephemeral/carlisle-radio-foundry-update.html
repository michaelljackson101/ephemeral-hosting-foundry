🎙️ A Quick Update for the Carlisle Ham Crew

Over the last two to three weeks down at the Kentucky Farm, I’ve been putting together a modern, software-driven ham radio control plane that we call Radio-Foundry.

Like many of us, I’ve often wanted a clean, reliable way to operate, monitor, and log across two different shacks—whether sitting in my home office in Carlisle, standing in the barn at the Kentucky farm with a smartphone, or traveling on the road.

Rather than relying on clunky, bandwidth-heavy remote desktop apps (like TeamViewer, AnyDesk, or VNC) that constantly freeze or drop audio, we built a browser-first, Linux-native “Radio-as-Code” ecosystem.

Here is a quick overview of what we’ve built, how it works under the hood, and where we’re taking it next!

🧭 The High-Level Architecture

At the heart of the system is an encrypted, zero-configuration Tailscale mesh network connecting low-power, headless Linux nodes (HP EliteDesk Minis and Dell Wyse sentinels) co-located with the radios at both locations.

⚡ What We’ve Built Over the Last 2–3 Weeks

1. 📱 Dual-Shack Remote Control via Mobile & Web

  • Zero Desktop Windows Needed: The station runs headlessly on Linux using hamlib (rigctld). Everything is accessible from a responsive web dashboard that fits right on a smartphone or tablet screen.
  • Instant QTH Switching: With a single dropdown in the sidebar, the control plane switches its profile, frequency channels, and CAT ports between Carlisle, MA and the Kentucky Farm.
  • Smart Physical Speaker Control: Built custom CAT attenuation so we can independently adjust or mute the physical speaker in the radio room while keeping clean digital audio streaming to our remote headset.

2. 🔌 Smart Power Sequencing & Remote Safety

Leaving transmitters and power supplies powered on 800 miles away can make anyone nervous. We implemented automated smart switching to solve this:

  • Remote Power Cut: Built smart REST/MQTT integration with Shelly In-Wall relays and Sonoff S31 smart switches to cleanly cut power to 13.8V linear/switching power supplies.
  • Graceful CAT Shutdown: Before cutting AC power, the system issues software CAT sleep commands (PS0;) to safely park the transceiver’s memory.
  • Failsafe Antenna Isolation: Integrated relay-driven coaxial disconnects so antennas automatically drop to hard earth ground when the station is powered down.

3. 📨 Digital Modes & Emergency Communications

  • Pat Winlink Web Client: Deployed a dedicated Winlink client daemon (pat-http) running on the shack Linux node. It integrates with rigctld for automated dial tuning across HF Winlink RMS gateways and local VHF packet nodes.
  • WSJT-X & FT8 Precision Timing: Installed WSJT-X alongside Chrony, delivering sub-microsecond (0.34µs) precision time synchronization across our network nodes for reliable weak-signal FT8/FT4 digital decoding.
  • Digital Voice Hotspot (SharkRF openSPOT4 Pro): Fully integrated automated status monitoring and profile switching on 434.900 MHz for cross-mode C4FM, DMR, and D-STAR connectivity.

4. 🌐 Live NetLogger & Scheduled Net Automation

  • Live NetLogger Cluster Scraper: Built a lightweight client that queries NetLogger’s real-time API. It displays all active on-air nets with band, mode, and check-in counts.
  • 1-Click “Tune to Net”: Spot a net in the dashboard (e.g., the local Marshall County Net or a regional 40m/80m traffic net), click “📡 Tune Radio”, and the rig instantly snaps to the exact net frequency and mode.
  • Calendar Sync: Connected with Fastmail CalDAV (MLJ Ham Radio calendar) to maintain a synchronized schedule of all our favorite weekly nets across New England and Kentucky.

5. 🤖 AI-First Ham Radio Assistant (Model Context Protocol)

One of the most exciting experimental pieces is our Amateur Radio MCP Server.

  • Allows local AI assistants to safely query our rigs, read S-meters, adjust VFOs, look up callsigns via QRZ XML, and transcribe audio from recorded nets (“Net Scribe”).
  • Everything is authenticated and sandboxed so it operates strictly within ham radio best practices and station safety limits.
